Cultural Heritage Risk Classifier

Automated classification of Colombian municipalities based on risk of losing cultural traditions using open demographic & cultural data.

Open Data Cultural Metrics 1,123 Municipalities

Business Understanding

Framing the problem, objectives, and strategic value

Context of the Problem

Colombia's vast cultural diversity — with over 102 indigenous groups, Afro-Colombian communities, and local peasant traditions — faces accelerated erosion due to urbanization, migration, globalization, and lack of safeguarding policies. Many municipalities lack quantitative indicators to measure the vitality of their intangible heritage (festivals, oral traditions, craftsmanship, rituals). Without data-driven risk classification, cultural policies are reactive, underfunded, or misdirected.

Key driver: The absence of an automatic, replicable system to identify municipalities where traditions are most vulnerable — before they disappear.

Business Objectives

  • Develop an automated ML classifier to assign each municipality a risk level (Low / Medium / High / Critical) of losing cultural traditions.
  • Leverage open demographic + cultural datasets (DANE, MinCultura, SIGOT) to ensure transparency and reproducibility.
  • Provide actionable dashboards for local governments and heritage NGOs to prioritize preservation budgets.
  • Reduce the reaction lag from years to months in identifying endangered traditions.

Questions the Model Must Answer

  • Which Colombian municipalities are at highest risk of cultural erosion based on current open data?
  • What combination of demographic (migration rate, median age, indigenous population decline) and cultural (number of annual festivals, active cultural groups, state investment) features best predicts risk?
  • How can risk levels be interpreted and visualized to support policy decisions?
  • Does the model identify municipalities with contradictory trends (e.g., low demographic risk but high cultural practice abandonment)?

Feasibility of the Project

Data availability: High. Open sources: DANE (population census, ethnicity, migration), Ministry of Culture (cultural infrastructure, festivals), ICANH (archaeological and living heritage records), and territorial development plans.

Technical feasibility: Medium-High. Supervised learning (Random Forest / XGBoost) or unsupervised clustering + risk indexing. Proven use cases for cultural vulnerability in UNESCO frameworks.

Implementation timeline: 8-12 weeks for first prototype (data ingestion → feature engineering → model training → dashboard).

Risks & Limitations

  • Incomplete or outdated data: Some municipalities have missing cultural indicators (e.g., last festival record older than 5 years).
  • Proxy bias: Using demographic variables (e.g., youth out-migration) as proxy for tradition loss may oversimplify qualitative heritage dynamics.
  • Labeling challenge: No existing official ground-truth labels; requires expert-based risk threshold definition or unsupervised anchoring.
  • Cultural sensitivity: Risk classification must not stigmatize communities; communication strategies are essential.

Expected Impact

  • Policy efficiency: Prioritize 20% of municipalities with critical risk for immediate cultural safeguarding programs.
  • Resource allocation: Redirect cultural investment from low-risk to high-risk areas, potentially saving ~30% of misallocated budgets.
  • Community empowerment: Provide local collectives with evidence-based arguments to demand heritage protection.
  • Long-term monitoring: Annual risk index updates to track effectiveness of preservation actions.

Real-world Justification

Colombia's National Development Plan (2022-2026) includes cultural preservation as a transversal axis, yet lacks quantitative municipal-level risk metrics. This project aligns with SDG 11.4 (protect cultural heritage) and the UNESCO 2003 Convention for Safeguarding Intangible Heritage.

In 2023, the Ministry of Culture reported that 43% of Afro-Colombian and indigenous traditions are practiced only by elders >60 years old. Without systematic risk classification, Colombia risks losing centuries-old knowledge within one generation.

Detailed Problem Description

Municipalities in Colombia experience heterogeneous cultural erosion. Current identification of at-risk traditions relies on subjective expert testimony or isolated ethnographic studies. No automated, scalable framework exists that fuses demographic open data (migration, age pyramids, ethnic composition) with cultural activity data (festival frequency, cultural organizations, public funding per capita). This leads to:

  • Delayed recognition of endangered practices (e.g., traditional weaving in Sucre, oral medicine in Vaupés).
  • Inefficient distribution of Programa Nacional de Estímulos (approx. COP $45 billion/year).
  • Lack of early warnings for intangible heritage disappearance.

Business Analysis

Stakeholders: Ministry of Culture (decision-makers), departmental secretaries of culture (operational), local indigenous councils (beneficiaries), academia (researchers), and NGOs (Heritage Watch).

Value proposition: A data-driven risk classifier that transforms raw demographic and cultural open data into strategic intelligence — reducing political bias in cultural budget allocation and enabling early intervention.

Success metrics (KPI):

  • Model accuracy ≥ 80% in predicting expert-validated risk zones.
  • At least 5 municipalities with critical risk receive new preservation programs within 6 months after pilot.
  • Dashboard adoption by 3 regional cultural secretariats.
